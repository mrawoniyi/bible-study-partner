# Prayer When You're Running on Empty

Nine days. Sam's material carries a single sustained thread — a year of entries circling the same
tiredness — so there is enough to work with, and the shift Sam named ("stop performing and still
feel like I'm allowed to be there") is a settled question rather than a quick one. Nine gives room
for the turning point to land in the middle and for a rule of life at the end that Sam can actually
keep while doing the evening drive.

## Days

- Day 0: Where You Actually Are — Name the present state honestly before trying to change it. What prayer has become, and how it got there. [full]
- Day 1: The Prayers That Are Only Groans — When you have no words, Scripture says something is still happening. Look at prayer that does not depend on your articulacy. [full]
- Day 2: Elijah Under the Broom Tree — A prophet asks to die and is given bread and sleep. What God does first with an exhausted person. [outline]
- Day 3: What You Think You're Bringing — Examine the unspoken transaction: what you believe you owe before you are welcome. [outline]
- Day 4: The Question You're Avoiding — The turning point. Ask directly whether God wants you, or wants your usefulness, and sit with the answer. [outline]
- Day 5: The Father Who Runs — The parable where the welcome arrives before the speech is finished. Read it as the older brother for once. [outline]
- Day 6: Small Prayers, Honest Ones — Prayer at the scale you actually have. Brevity as faithfulness rather than failure. [outline]
- Day 7: A Rule of Life You Can Keep — Build something that survives a bad week. Concrete, small, and yours. [outline]
- Day 8: What You Found — Integration. Gather the insights, name the patterns, and land the commitments. [outline]

The turning point sits at Day 4, the middle of the nine. Day 7 is the rule-of-life day, so
Application is promoted into Tier 1 there. Day 8 is integration, so Pattern Recognition is promoted
into Tier 1. Context Builder is scheduled on Days 0, 2, 5 and 8 — four of nine, inside the 25–50%
band, rotating the angle each time.

## Day 0 — Where You Actually Are

### Opening reflection
Before a plan tries to move you anywhere, it is worth being accurate about where you are standing.
That sounds obvious, and it is the step most of us skip, because naming the present state out loud
costs something. You have been praying every day for nine months. You have also written that it
feels like reciting a list while tired. Both of those are true at once, and holding them together
without explaining either one away is the work of today.

There is no diagnosis coming. Today is a description. What prayer has become, when it changed, and
what you would want instead if nobody were grading the answer.

### Bible passages
- **Psalm 62:5-8** — A psalm that tells the soul what to do rather than reporting how it feels, and
  ends by inviting the plain truth to be poured out.
- **Exodus 33:12-14** — Moses argues with God about presence, and does it without a shred of
  politeness. Worth noticing how much candour the text allows.
- **Matthew 11:28-30** — The invitation is issued specifically to people who are worn out. Read it
  today as a description of who it is addressed to, not as a promise you have to qualify for.

### Reflection from your journey
In June you wrote that prayer had "turned into a list I recite while I'm tired." In September, that
nothing was landing. Those two lines are nine months apart and they are the same sentence said
twice, which usually means something has settled rather than passed. You also said you are wary of
admitting the distance in case it sounds like unbelief.

So let's say it here, where nobody is listening for heresy. The distance is real, you have been
faithful inside it, and neither of those cancels the other. That is the ground we are starting from.

### Questions
1. [Personal Connection] When you pray in the car on the way home, what are you actually doing? Not
   what you intend it to be — what it is.
2. [Context Builder] Your grandmother prayed out loud in the kitchen and you remember it as normal.
   What did you absorb from that about what prayer is for?
3. [Tension] You wrote that you would be more comfortable if God needed something from you. What is
   uncomfortable about being wanted rather than needed?
4. [Observation] In Exodus 33, Moses says he will not move without God's presence. What do you
   notice about how that conversation is allowed to go?

### Prayer prompts
- **Begin**: "This is where I am." Say the one sentence you would not normally say out loud.
- **Practice**: Five minutes, no requests. Not silence as a technique, just five minutes where you
  are not asking for anything. If your mind goes to your father, let it; notice whether the asking
  starts again by itself, and let that be information rather than a failure.
- **Close**: Pray Psalm 62:8 back, in your own words, naming the thing you are pouring out.

### End of session
When you've finished today's reflections and prayers, say **"I'm done with Day 0"** and I'll save
your answers and pull out what matters for tomorrow.

## Day 1 — The Prayers That Are Only Groans

### Opening reflection
Yesterday you said the car prayers are "mostly narration, half-aimed" — that you talk through the
day and only realise at the end that you meant it as prayer. You said it slightly apologetically.
Today's texts take that seriously rather than correcting it.

Romans 8 makes an odd claim: that when you do not know what to pray, something is praying anyway,
underneath the level of words. It is the opposite of how most of us were trained to think about
prayer, where articulacy is the skill and fluency is the proof. Hold that claim next to what you
described yesterday and see what it does to it.

### Bible passages
- **Romans 8:26-27** — Prayer that does not depend on your having the words.
- **Psalm 42:1-5** — A psalm that says the honest thing first and only then tells itself the true
  thing. Notice the order.
- **Mark 9:24** — Nine words, and they contradict themselves, and they are accepted.

### Reflection from your journey
You measure prayer against a weekend when you were nineteen and felt completely seen. That is a
high bar and a specific one: it makes fluency and feeling the test. Yesterday you noticed something
slightly different, that the half-aimed narration in the car is "the only honest praying I do." You
said it as a confession. I want to leave it standing as a finding for a day and see whether it
holds.

### Questions
1. [Personal Connection] If the groaning counts, what changes about the drive home tonight?
2. [Tension] Which would be harder to accept — that your best prayers are the fluent ones, or that
   they are the ones you barely notice making?
3. [Reframe] Suppose the weekend at nineteen was not the standard but one instance. What would you
   have to give up to believe that?

### Prayer prompts
- **Begin**: Mark 9:24, as written. Do not improve it.
- **Practice**: Ten minutes in the car with the radio off. No agenda, no list. If words come, fine;
  if not, that is the point of the exercise.
- **Close**: Psalm 42:5, addressed to yourself, out loud.

### End of session
When you've finished, say **"I'm done with Day 1"**.
