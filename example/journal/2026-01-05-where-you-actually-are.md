# Where You Actually Are

Day 0 of Prayer When You're Running on Empty. Standard session, about twenty minutes. Three
passages, two explored. The session turned on the first question: Sam described the car prayers as
"the only honest praying I do" and then apologised for it, which became the thread for the rest of
the evening.

## Bible references
- Psalm 62:5-8
- Exodus 33:12-14
- Matthew 11:28-30

## My reflection: Psalm 62:5-8
I noticed he's telling himself what to do. "Yes, my soul, find rest in God." That's not what I do —
I wait to feel it and then I'm annoyed when I don't. He's instructing himself. I don't know if
that's discipline or just talking yourself round.

## Psalm 62:5-8 — historical and literary context
The psalm sits in a run of David's psalms written under pressure from people he could not trust,
and the Hebrew behind "find rest" carries the sense of silence or stillness rather than relief from
work. Structurally the psalm repeats its opening statement almost word for word in the middle, which
is the psalmist saying the same thing twice on purpose — once as a claim, and once as an instruction
after the complaint has been aired. The pouring out in verse 8 comes after both, not before.

## My reflection: Exodus 33:12-14
He's arguing. That's what struck me. He more or less says I'm not going anywhere unless you come
with us, and that's allowed. Nobody taught me that was allowed.

## Matthew 11:28-30 — theological context
The invitation is addressed to a specific group: those already carrying and already tired. In the
first-century setting a rabbi's "yoke" meant his whole way of reading and keeping the law, so the
offer is not the absence of a yoke but a different one, and the qualification for being offered it
is exhaustion rather than competence.

## When you pray in the car on the way home, what are you actually doing?
Honestly? Narrating. I talk through the day, what happened at Dad's, what the carer said, what I
need to sort tomorrow. It's half-aimed. I only notice at the end that I meant it as prayer. It's
probably the only honest praying I do, which is a bit sad when you say it out loud.

## What did you absorb from your grandmother praying out loud in the kitchen about what prayer is for?
That it wasn't an event. She didn't stop what she was doing. Nobody went quiet. It was just running
in the background of the house. Church prayer was the opposite — there was a right way to sound and
I learned that quite young. I think I've been doing church prayer for twenty years and calling the
kitchen one a lapse.

## You wrote that you'd be more comfortable if God needed something from you. What's uncomfortable about being wanted rather than needed?
[long pause] Because needed is legible. If I'm useful I know where I stand. I can check. Wanted is
just a claim somebody makes and you either take it or you don't, and I don't know how to verify it,
so I end up doing more instead.

## Prayer: Begin — say the one sentence you wouldn't normally say out loud
"I've been doing this for nine months and I don't know if anyone's on the other end."

## Prayer: Practice — five minutes asking for nothing
Managed about three. Went to Dad twice. The second time I noticed I'd started asking again without
deciding to. Didn't beat myself up about it, which is new.

## Prayer: Close — Psalm 62:8 in your own words
Whatever this is, I'm bringing it as it is. I'm not tidying it up first.
